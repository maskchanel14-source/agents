The 12-point Stocky exit checklist

What to export, in what order, while Shopify still allows read-only access. Shopify guarantees that access for at least 90 days after 31 August 2026 — a floor, not a date — and states that suppliers can't be exported at all. Vendor lead times, carton multiples and MOQs only exist inside purchase order history, so pull those first.

Last updated: October 2026 · maintained by Small Signal Studio

01Export the four files that matter

#ReportWhereWhy
1Purchase orders (full history)Stocky → Purchase Orders → ExportThe only source of vendor lead times and carton multiples
2Products / inventoryShopify Admin → Products → ExportSKU, cost, barcode, on-hand
3Sales by SKU, 90–180 daysShopify Analytics → Sales by product variantDaily velocity and ABC tiers
4Supplier listStocky → Suppliers (screenshot or manual sheet)Names, emails, currency, terms

Name them po_history.csv, products.csv, sales_by_sku.csv, suppliers.csv. Don't rename columns yet.

02Rebuild lead time from timestamps

For each vendor, take every historical PO and compute received_date − created_date. You want three numbers, not one:

  • mean — for reorder points
  • standard deviation — the safety-stock buffer
  • maximum observed — the worst case you plan around

With fewer than three historical POs, mark the lead time as “assumed” and revisit after two cycles. Never average a single data point.

03Infer carton multiples

Look at ordered quantities per SKU per PO. If every order is a multiple of 12 or 24, that's the case pack. Round future quantities up to it — ordering 47 when the pack is 12 means you are short, not clever.

04Capture MOQ twice: units and dollars

Vendors enforce two minimums and merchants satisfy one and forget the other:

  • MOQ units — minimum pieces per SKU or per order
  • Minimum order value — often tied to free freight

The top-up rule that pays for itself: if a PO is short of the free-freight threshold by less than the freight cost, add A-tier fast movers to close the gap. Same cash out, no freight, more cover on your best sellers.

05Build the 12-column supplier sheet

This schema replaces what Stocky stored. It is more valuable than any app:

vendor | vendor_email | currency | lead_time_days | lead_time_stdev_days
case_pack | moq_units | moq_dollars | free_freight_dollars
payment_terms | incoterm | sku_list

06Correct velocity for stockout days

units sold ÷ days is wrong for any SKU that was out of stock — sold-out days drag the average down and you under-order your best seller:

velocity = units_sold / (days - stockout_days)

07Set safety stock by service level, not by vibes

safety_stock = Z × σ_demand × √(lead_time)     # Z = 1.65 for 95%, 2.05 for 98%
reorder_point = (velocity × lead_time) + safety_stock

08Classify SKUs A/B/C by revenue

A-tier (top 80% of revenue): never stock out, top up POs to protect them. B-tier: normal rules. C-tier: order only to fill a carton, never to fill a pallet.

09Generate one Draft PO by hand

Before trusting any tool — this one included — build one vendor's PO manually and compare. The differences are the rules that lived only in your buyer's head.

10Start a receiving log now

Three-way matching needs three documents: PO, dock receiving count, supplier invoice. You have the first and third. The receiving count is the one nobody keeps:

po_number | sku | qty_received | date | damaged

11Audit one past invoice

Pick the largest invoice from the last 90 days and check three things: unit cost vs PO, quantity received vs invoiced, freight vs agreed. Do it once and the workflow explains itself.

12Back the exports up twice

Keep local and cloud copies outside Shopify. The read-only window closes; your drive does not.
