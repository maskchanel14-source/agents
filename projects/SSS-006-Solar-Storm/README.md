# SSS-006 — The solar storm that blacked out a grid

1989 Québec blackout, scientifically scoped to known geomagnetic and power-grid effects, not a prediction of global catastrophe. NOAA source and detailed footage credits/rights in `publishing-kit.txt`. Eight unique source clips are used once each: genuine observations or filmed footage, not AI visualizations. The other-date solar sequences and auroras are explicitly labelled illustrative; the research mosaic is a 6 fps scientific timelapse and is not presented as live footage. Speech is synthesized. Score and effects are procedural original audio.

The master is vertical 1080×1920/60fps but footage remains uncropped within it; a blurred full-height extension fills the portrait background. Most source clips are 1920×1080/24fps, the solar SDO observations are 1024×1024/24fps and are displayed without upscale, the science mosaic is 1920×1080/6fps. The master frame rate does not make the source imagery native 60fps. This uses eight sources and eight long shots, below the SOP's 12–30 sources / 34–36 shots; constraints were prioritized over fake footage and repeats. Captions and word highlights use proportional timing rather than alignment, and must be checked by listening; audibility needs creator review.

To reproduce, create `/tmp/vesper` from `https://github.com/itsaligadimi/Vesper` at `ec230c9ead2be84178652688c4e7dd31d8aa6e72`, `/tmp/solar-movies` from `https://github.com/beeswaxpat/lumara-sun-movie` branch `gh-pages` commit `afac92e918141e370a5a97c18b029001681bbc19`, `/tmp/helio` from `https://github.com/sr-dash/heliofield-movies` branch `site` at `67010363a7db5ca9afba56f445a24076babadd28`; source material is not committed. The solar source includes observations from November 8–9; the edit uses only its opening approximately 14 hours (November 8 UTC), not later observations. Place narration b1.wav–b8.wav in `/home/user/sss006/audio`, bootstrap media packages, run `build_audio.py`, then `render.py`. Narrator selection is session-scoped.
