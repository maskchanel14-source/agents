2. YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION (FIRST 2 LINES HOOK + CITATIONS + 4 HASHTAGS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Nobody programmed this industrial robot arm to throw apples or scrub mustard off plates. It learned directly from a $73 3D-printed plastic claw strapped to a GoPro.

Normally, teaching a robot arm requires bolting a $40,000 machine to a single lab table and inching it around with a joystick at 35 demos per hour. Researchers at Stanford, Columbia, MIT, and Toyota Research Institute flipped the pipeline with UMI (Universal Manipulation Interface):

• Hardware: $73 3D-printed parallel-jaw claw (780 g, 80 mm continuous finger stroke) + $298 GoPro & mount ($371 total).
• Implicit Stereo Vision: Two tiny angled side mirrors beside a 155-degree GoPro fisheye lens split one camera into virtual stereo eyes for 3D depth, while the built-in IMU gyroscope tracks rapid wrist snaps via visual-inertial SLAM.
• Speed: 111 demonstrations per hour in real kitchens, dorms, and outdoor cafes (3.2x faster than SpaceMouse teleoperation) with zero robots present during data collection.
• Zero-Shot Transfer: Mounting the exact same claw onto different arms (6-DoF UR5e and 7-DoF Franka) with millisecond inference-time latency matching unlocks dynamic mid-air tossing, bimanual sweater folding, 7-step dishwashing (even when humans squirt extra mustard mid-wash or turn off the lights), and 70% zero-shot success in unseen environments—including a flowing outdoor water fountain.

Primary Source:
