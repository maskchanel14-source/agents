## 2. Description (copy-paste)

> Betelgeuse is roughly 700 times wider than the Sun. Put it where the Sun is and its surface would swallow Mars. And something is orbiting *through* it.
>
> Six years of radio observations with the VLA and ALMA (22–485 GHz, seven bands) show the giant's disc going out of round, then round again, in a way that repeats with orbital phase. ## 5. Pinned comment

> The part most videos will skip: **we have never photographed this companion.** It is inferred from the shape of Betelgeuse's radio disc across 2021–2026 — the disc becomes elliptical near maximum separation and relaxes back. A companion at ~2 stellar radii explains that cleanly, and it would be 10–100× fainter than the maps can resolve, which is exactly why nobody has an image of it.
>
> Two honesty notes: (1) the ~2,100-day cycle is a **proposed** periodicity, not a confirmed ephemeris — that's why the narration says "if the model holds." (2) The companion dot on screen is drawn much larger than a ~1 solar-mass star would actually appear at this scale. The orbit distance is real; the dot size is not.
>
> Also: Betelgeuse's distance is quoted anywhere from ~500 to ~650 light-years depending on method, so this video doesn't quote one at all.
>
> Would you rather know the exact date a star will explode — or never know? 👇
