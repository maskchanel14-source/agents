## 2. Description (copy-paste)

> In 1765 an Italian doctor described a man who could not sleep — and died. Two hundred years later, one family was still dying the same way. The cause turned out to be a single letter of DNA: a missense mutation at codon 178 of the gene for the prion protein. That protein is in your brain right now.
>
> Fatal familial insomnia is inherited, autosomal dominant, and 100% fatal. Half of every generation carries it. It destroys the thalamus — a switchboard the size of a walnut that decides when you sleep — and takes your sleep away in stages: spindles first, then deep sleep, then dreaming. Patients stay awake for months while their body burns fuel it cannot store. Median survival is eighteen months. There is no treatment, and no survivors have ever been recorded.
>
> About seventy families are known. And it is not infectious. It is you.
>
> This is a prion disease, but not the one you have heard of. No DNA, no virus, no cure — just a protein that folds wrong and takes its neighbours with it.
>
> Sources: Lugaresi et al., NEJM 1986 · Medori et al., NEJM 1992 · Montagna et al., Lancet Neurology 2003 · StatPearls NBK482208 · OMIM #600072. Full claim-by-claim sourcing and licences in the pinned comment.
